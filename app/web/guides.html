Make water safe to drink

Start at the top. The first question decides whether the water can be treated at all.

Could the water have fuel, toxic chemicals or radioactive material in it?For example it smells of fuel or chemicals, or it is floodwater.
↓
Yes, or not sure
Do not drink it. Stop here.Boiling and disinfecting cannot make this water safe. Use bottled water or a different source, and ask your health department for advice.
No
Carry on.The steps below kill germs. They do not remove chemicals.
↓
Is the water cloudy?
↓
Yes
Filter it through a clean cloth, paper towel or coffee filter.Or let it settle and draw off the clear water.
No
Go to the next step.
↓
If you have a portable water filter, run the water through it.Follow the filter's instructions. Portable filters do not remove viruses, so always disinfect afterwards.
↓
Then kill the germs. Boiling is the best way. Pick one:
↓
BoilBring clear water to a rolling boil for 1 minute. Above 6,500 feet, boil for 3 minutes. Let it cool, then store it in clean, covered containers.
Bleach (unscented household bleach)If the label has instructions for drinking water, follow them. If not: 8 drops per gallon for 5% to 9% bleach. Double it if the water is cloudy, colored or very cold. Stir and let it stand at least 30 minutes.
↓
Used bleach? Check for a slight chlorine smell.If there is none, repeat the dose and let it stand another 15 minutes before use.

Bleach does not kill some parasites as well as boiling does, including Cryptosporidium and Giardia. Official instructions from your health department or water utility always win.

Sources: CDC, "How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency" (reviewed Sept. 19, 2024). EPA, "Emergency Disinfection of Drinking Water" (updated Feb. 24, 2026). EPA's table gives 8 drops per gallon for 6% bleach and 6 drops for 8.25% bleach. This guide was checked against both in Nov. 2026.

Trauma first aid: MARCH

Work left to right. Don't move on until the step is handled. Bleeding kills fastest, so it comes first.

M

Massive bleeding

Hard direct pressure. Limb spurting or soaking through: tourniquet 2 to 3 inches above the wound, tight until bleeding stops. Write the time on it.

A

Airway

Talking means the airway is open. If not: head tilt, chin lift. Unconscious and breathing: recovery position on their side.

R

Respiration

Watch the chest rise. Chest wound with air sucking in: cover it with plastic taped on 3 sides.

C

Circulation

Pale, cold, sweaty, fast pulse means shock. Lay flat, raise legs if no spine injury, keep warm. Not breathing: start CPR.

H

Hypothermia and head

Keep them warm even in mild weather. Watch for confusion, vomiting or unequal pupils after a head hit.

Source: Stop the Bleed and TCCC "MARCH" order. For full detail open Where There Is No Doctor (Books tab).
